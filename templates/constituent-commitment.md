# Constituent Commitment — Template

The only oath in this project. A candidate signs this **to their voters** — never to a party, a platform, or this project.

Publish it, dated and signed, on launch day. Grade it publicly after election day and at every anniversary in office.

---

## My Commitment to the Voters of [District / State]

I, **[Name]**, am running for **[Office]** as an independent candidate. I am not running as a member of any party, and I owe my seat — if you give it to me — to you alone.

If elected, I commit to the following **specific, checkable** actions:

1. **[Commitment 1]** — e.g. "Hold an open town hall in this district every month I am in office, announced at least 7 days ahead."
2. **[Commitment 2]** — e.g. "Vote against any budget that [specific, measurable condition], and publish my reason within 24 hours of every major vote."
3. **[Commitment 3]** — e.g. "Deliver [specific local project / fix] by [date], and publish progress quarterly, including when it falls behind."
4. **[Add or remove lines — 3 to 7 total. Vague is worthless. If it can't be checked, rewrite it until it can.]**

**How to hold me to this:**

- My scorecard lives at **[URL]**, updated by **[date / frequency]**
- If I break a commitment, I will say so there, in writing, with the reason — before anyone has to catch me
- I will not take a party whip's instruction on any vote. My votes and my reasons will be published in plain language.

**What I will not do:**

- I will not join a party caucus as a condition of support, funding, or committee placement
- I will not sell or hand over your contact information, ever
- I will not run for this seat on a different promise than this one

Signed: ______________________________  Date: ______________

---

## Drafting rules (delete before publishing)

- **Specific beats inspiring.** "Fight for working families" is not a commitment. A date, a number, and a place are.
- **Fewer, harder promises.** 5 kept commitments beat 25 slogans.
- **Include one promise that will cost you something.** That's how voters know it's real.
- **Write the failure clause yourself.** Candidates who pre-commit to admitting misses are trusted longer.
