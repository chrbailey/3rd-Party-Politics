# California — Independent / Unaffiliated Ballot Access

Office covered: U.S. House, U.S. Senate, statewide constitutional offices (Governor, Lt. Governor, Secretary of State, Controller, Treasurer, Attorney General, Insurance Commissioner), State Senate, Member of the State Assembly, State Board of Equalization.

## ⚠️ Read this first — California is not a petition-to-the-general-election state

Since Proposition 14 (Top Two Candidates Open Primary Act, effective Jan 1, 2011), all candidates for **voter-nominated offices** — congressional, state legislative, and state constitutional offices — appear on **one primary ballot**, and only the **top two vote-getters advance to the general election, regardless of party preference**. An independent / No Party Preference (NPP) candidate gets on the ballot by filing for the **primary**, not by petitioning straight onto the general-election ballot.

The old "independent nomination" route (Elections Code §§ 8300–8550, with the 1% / 3% signature thresholds quoted in pre-2011 SOS documents) survives only as a **general-election-only fallback** when no candidate was nominated at the primary for that office — see "General-election-only independent nomination" below. Do not use the 1% / 3% figures as the requirement for a normal independent run.

**Cycle note:** figures below are verified against the California Secretary of State's **2026 Election Guide** (June 2, 2026 primary; Nov 3, 2026 general — both now past as of this verification). Re-verify against the 2028 guide when SOS publishes it; fees and dates change each cycle.

## Requirements (primary route — the normal path for an independent)

- Filing form(s): Nomination papers + Declaration of Candidacy (includes Oath of Office and Political Party Preference History), Ballot Designation Worksheet, Code of Fair Campaign Practices Statement (voluntary). Governor candidates also file 5 years of income tax returns + disclosure consent (§§ 8902–8903). Forms are issued free by the county elections official of the candidate's county of residence (for U.S. House, any county in the district if the candidate does not reside in it).
- Signature requirement (nomination papers, Elections Code § 8062): statewide office / U.S. Senate: **65–100** registered voters; U.S. House, State Senate, State Assembly, Board of Equalization: **40–60**. Signers must be registered voters in the district; for a voter-nominated office, signers **need not** have disclosed a preference for any party (§ 8068). No more signers may be secured than the maximum (§ 8067).
- Signature gathering window: nomination papers available **Feb 9, 2026 (E-113)** and must be delivered by **March 6, 2026, 5:00 p.m. (E-88)**. In-lieu-of-filing-fee petitions available from **Dec 19, 2025 (E-165)**, last day to submit **Feb 4, 2026 (E-118)**. In-lieu signatures count toward the nomination-paper requirement (§ 8061) — if the in-lieu petition alone meets the nomination number, no separate nomination papers are needed, but a Declaration of Candidacy must still be filed in the nomination period.
- Filing deadline: **March 6, 2026, 5:00 p.m.** (E-88) — declaration of candidacy and nomination papers. Miss it and the candidate is out for the cycle; there is no later independent filing for voter-nominated offices that were contested in the primary.
- Filing fee (or fee-waiver / in-lieu signatures) — 2026 cycle, SOS Guide § 3-1 (§§ 8062, 8103, 8106(a), 8162(b); salaries as of Dec 1, 2025; fees are nonrefundable):

  | Office | Fee | In-lieu signatures | Nomination signatures |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Governor | $4,918.58 (2% of $245,929) | 6,000 | 65–100 |
  | Lt. Governor / Secretary of State | $3,688.94 (2%) | 6,000 | 65–100 |
  | Controller / Treasurer / Insurance Commissioner | $3,934.86 (2%) | 6,000 | 65–100 |
  | Attorney General / Superintendent of Public Instruction | $4,272.34 (2%) | 6,000 | 65–100 |
  | U.S. House | $1,740.00 (1% of $174,000) | 1,714 | 40–60 |
  | State Senate | $1,346.94 (1% of $134,694) | 1,714 | 40–60 |
  | State Assembly | $1,346.94 (1%) | 857 | 40–60 |
  | Board of Equalization | $1,844.47 (1%) | 4,743 | 40–60 |

  Each in-lieu signature has a monetary value (fee ÷ in-lieu signatures); a candidate may cover all or part of the fee with signatures.
- Party-affiliation cutoff: the candidate declares a party preference — or lack of one — on the Declaration of Candidacy, consistent with their most recent affidavit of registration (Elections Code § 8002.5). **UNVERIFIED for the current cycle:** whether any minimum lookback applies before a candidate may declare No Party Preference (the pre-2011 § 8550 affiliation rule is not carried forward here — see below).
- Sore-loser rule: a candidate who was on the ballot as a party candidate at the direct primary and was defeated for that party's nomination is **ineligible** for nomination as an independent candidate, and also ineligible as a party central committee's fill-in for the general election (Elections Code § 8003(a)). No person may file nomination papers for both a party nomination and an independent nomination for the same office, or for more than one office at the same election (§ 8003(b)).
- Ballot label allowed: a candidate with no qualified party preference is listed as **"Party Preference: None"** — not "Independent." (SOS: "If a candidate does not have a qualified political party preference, 'Party Preference: None' will be indicated by the candidate's name on the ballot.")

## General-election-only independent nomination (fallback, §§ 8300+)

A candidate for a nonpartisan or voter-nominated office **for which no candidate was nominated at the primary** may be nominated subsequent to or in lieu of a primary under Part 2 of Division 8, beginning at § 8300 (SOS Guide § 3-4, citing § 8300).

- Signature requirement under § 8400: **UNVERIFIED for the 2026/2028 cycle.** Older SOS documents (2006, 2010) used 1% of registered voters for statewide offices and 3% for U.S. House / legislative / Board of Equalization districts, based on the registration report before the preceding general election — but those predate Prop 14 and are **not** carried forward as current figures here. Verify against the SOS general-election guide / current § 8400 text before using any number.
- Affiliation cutoff under § 8550: **UNVERIFIED for the current cycle.** The 2010 SOS guide stated a candidate could not have been registered with a qualified party for roughly the preceding year; that statement is stale and is not asserted here.
- Sore-loser rule for this route: § 8003(a) as above, plus § 8301 (cannot have filed as a partisan candidate at the primary and been defeated).

## Gotchas

- ⚠️ **The top-two primary is the whole ballgame.** An independent who misses the March filing deadline (2026: Mar 6, 5 p.m.) does not get a second chance via a general-election petition for any office that was contested in the primary.
- ⚠️ **"Independent" is not a ballot label in California.** The ballot says "Party Preference: None." Campaign materials calling the candidate "Independent" are fine; the ballot will not say it.
- ⚠️ **Do not quote the 1% / 3% numbers as current.** They appear in old SOS PDFs and secondary sources, but they belong to the pre-Prop 14 independent-nomination scheme / the § 8300 fallback, and the current thresholds for that fallback are marked UNVERIFIED above.
- In-lieu signatures do double duty (§ 8061): gather enough and they satisfy the nomination-paper count too — but the Declaration of Candidacy is still required.
- A candidate may file nomination papers for only one office at the same election (§ 8003(b)).
- Filing fees are nonrefundable (§§ 8105, 8106).
