# Washington — Independent / Unaffiliated Ballot Access

Office covered: U.S. House, U.S. Senate, statewide executive offices (Governor, Lt. Governor, Secretary of State, Treasurer, Auditor, Attorney General, Superintendent of Public Instruction, Commissioner of Public Lands, Insurance Commissioner), State Senate, State House of Representatives.

## ⚠️ Read this first — Washington is a top-two state, like California

Since Initiative 872 (2004, upheld by the U.S. Supreme Court in 2008), all candidates for **partisan office** — congressional, state legislative, and statewide executive — appear on **one primary ballot**, and only the **top two vote-getters advance to the general election, regardless of party preference**. An independent / unaffiliated candidate gets on the ballot by filing a Declaration of Candidacy for the **primary**, not by petitioning straight onto the general-election ballot.

There is **no separate independent nominating petition** for these offices. The only petition in the process is the **filing-fee petition** (see below): a candidate who cannot pay the filing fee may substitute signatures, one signature per dollar of fee. Do not confuse that with a ballot-access petition — every candidate, party or independent, files the same way.

Washington also **does not register voters by party**, so there is no party-affiliation cutoff to satisfy (see below).

**Cycle note:** figures below are verified against the Washington Secretary of State's **2026 State Candidates Guide** (primary Aug 4, 2026; general Nov 3, 2026 — both now past as of this verification). Re-verify against the 2028 guide when SOS publishes it; fees follow salaries, which change each July 1, and dates change each cycle.

## Requirements (primary route — the only path for an independent)

- Filing form(s): Declaration of Candidacy, filed online at sos.wa.gov/elections/file-office (recommended), by mail, or in person with the Secretary of State (federal, statewide, state legislative, court of appeals, and superior court offices) or the county elections office (all other offices). The declaration includes the candidate's ballot name, party preference (see below), and oath. A Voters' Pamphlet profile is a separate submission (deadline May 19, 2026, 4:30 p.m.).
- Signature requirement: **none as a ballot-access threshold.** There is no nomination-signature count for U.S. House, State Legislature, statewide, or U.S. Senate in Washington. The only signature number is the filing-fee petition, below.
- Signature gathering window: filing-fee petitions must be brought to the filing officer before filing, and candidates submitting petitions may not file electronically; no additional signatures are accepted after the initial submission, and a petition is valid only for that filing period/election. **UNVERIFIED for the current cycle:** whether fee-petition signatures may be gathered before the filing period opens.
- Filing deadline: **Friday, May 8, 2026, 5:00 p.m.** — online filing opens Monday, May 4, 2026, at 8:00 a.m.; mailed declarations accepted from April 20, 2026. Last day to withdraw: **Monday, May 11, 2026, 5:00 p.m.** Miss the filing deadline and the candidate is out for the cycle; there is no later independent filing for partisan offices.
- Filing fee (or fee-waiver / in-lieu signatures) — RCW 29A.24.091 and 29A.24.131; fee = **1% of the office's fixed annual salary at the time of filing** (offices with salary of $1,000 or less: $10). The dollar figures below are the **VoteWA Scheduled Races list** (2026 list for House/Legislature; 2028 list, the next cycle with those offices on the ballot, for Senate/statewide), verified 2026-11-06 — they are 1% of the salary **in effect during filing week (May 2026)**, i.e. the July 1, 2025 schedule, *not* the July 1, 2026 schedule. (The July 1, 2026 salaries — Governor $234,275, legislators $72,494 — took effect *after* the May filing week, so they do not set the 2026 fees.) Fees are nonrefundable, even on withdrawal or filing for the wrong office:

  \* Signature counts are the whole-dollar part of the fee: the petition must contain "not less than a number of signatures … equal to the number of dollars of the filing fee" (RCW 29A.24.091(4)), and only signatures of registered voters in the jurisdiction of the office count. These counts are derived from the SOS "one signature per whole dollar" rule — the SOS does not publish a separate per-office signature table. A candidate may **not** combine petition signatures and money to satisfy the fee (2026 Guide, p. 4). The 2028 statewide/Senate fee amounts are as currently listed for 2028 and could change if salaries change before that filing period.
- Party-affiliation cutoff: **none — Washington does not register voters by party.** A candidate simply states a party preference on the Declaration of Candidacy, or states no preference. No lookback, no disaffiliation period, and no party approval is required to state a preference.
- Sore-loser rule: a candidate who loses in the primary — as a regular printed candidate or as a declared write-in — **cannot file in the general election as a write-in for the same race** (RCW 29A.24.311 and RCW 29A.60.021, per the SOS Write-in Candidates page). Double filing is also barred: no person may file for more than one office, except precinct committee officer or temporary positions such as charter review board member or freeholder (RCW 29A.24.075). A write-in candidate may file for either the primary or the general, but not both for the same office.
- Ballot label allowed: a candidate's stated preference appears as **"(Prefers [party])"** after the name; a candidate who states no preference appears as **"(States No Party Preference)"**. The preference is for voter information only and does not imply nomination, endorsement, or approval by that party. A candidate may state a preference of up to **18 characters**, first letter of each word capitalized (acronyms/initialisms in all caps); it may not be edited after the withdrawal deadline, and no changes are accepted between primary and general. **UNVERIFIED:** whether SOS accepts the literal word "Independent" as a preference string in the current filing portal.

## Top-two advance rule

- Primary: **Tuesday, August 4, 2026**. General: **Tuesday, November 3, 2026**.
- The two candidates with the most votes in the primary advance, regardless of party preference, and a candidate must receive **at least 1% of the votes cast in that race** to advance (SOS Top Two FAQ). If only one or two candidates file, the race skips the primary and appears only on the general ballot.

## Gotchas

- ⚠️ **Do NOT use 500 / 750 / 1,000 as Washington's signature requirements.** Those numbers come from **SB 5225 (2025)**, a bill that would have required signature petitions of 500 (legislature), 750 (U.S. House), and 1,000 (statewide / U.S. Senate) plus split filing-fee payment. It is **not** in the current RCW 29A.24.091 as crawled 2026-11-06: current law has no ballot-access signature petition at all, and the fee petition is signatures equal to the dollar amount of the fee (e.g. 1,740 for a House run). Secondary sources quoting the 500/750/1,000 figures are quoting the bill, not the law.
- ⚠️ **The top-two primary is the whole ballgame, as in California.** An independent who misses the May filing week does not get a second chance via a general-election petition for any partisan office.
- ⚠️ **"Independent" is a preference, not a party.** Washington voters are not registered by party, so there is no disaffiliation clock — but the ballot will say "(Prefers …)" or "(States No Party Preference)", and a preference does not mean the party nominated or endorsed the candidate.
- ⚠️ **Fee petitions are all-or-nothing.** You cannot mix signatures and cash for the fee, no extra signatures are accepted after the first submission, petition filers cannot file electronically, and the fee is nonrefundable even if you withdraw.
- ⚠️ **Fees are set by the salary in effect at filing time, not the salary on election day.** The July 1, 2026 salary schedule (Governor $234,275, legislators $72,494) took effect *after* the May 2026 filing week, so it does not set the 2026 fees — the VoteWA amounts in the table above are the 2026-cycle figures. A 2028 filing fee will differ again; use the VoteWA Scheduled Races list for that cycle.
- Presidential ballot access is a different track (minor-party / independent presidential candidates: nominating convention + 1,000-signature petition, per the SOS 2024 presidential guide) and is **not** covered here; 2026 is a midterm year with no presidential race on the ballot.
