# Texas — Independent / Unaffiliated Ballot Access

Office covered: U.S. Senate, U.S. House, statewide offices (Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Attorney General, Comptroller, Commissioner of the General Land Office, Commissioner of Agriculture, Railroad Commissioner, Supreme Court Justice, Court of Criminal Appeals Judge), State Senate, State Representative, State Board of Education, Court of Appeals, District Judge, District Attorney, and county/precinct offices (County Judge, County Clerk, Sheriff, County Commissioner, Constable, Justice of the Peace, etc.).

## ⚠️ Read this first — Texas is a two-step, petition-only state with a compressed window

Texas does **not** let an independent pay a filing fee instead of petitioning, and it does **not** let an independent start collecting signatures until the parties have finished their primaries. There is also a mandatory first step — a Declaration of Intent — months before the petition deadline. Miss the declaration and the later application is rejected outright.

**Cycle note:** figures below are verified against the Texas Secretary of State's **2026 independent-candidate guide** (primary March 3, 2026; primary runoff May 26, 2026; general November 3, 2026 — all now past as of this verification). Re-verify against the 2028 guide when SOS publishes it; the percentage thresholds in the Election Code persist, but the absolute statewide signature number will change because it is pegged to the most recent gubernatorial vote — after the 2026 governor's race, the basis is no longer the 2022 vote.

## Requirements

- Filing form(s): (1) Declaration of Intent to Run as an Independent Candidate, filed with the Secretary of State (district and state offices) or the county judge (county or precinct offices); (2) candidate application for a place on the general-election ballot, accompanied by a nominating petition. Candidates for federal office file a separate federal application; judicial candidates file additional information under § 141.0311.
- Signature requirement (nominating petition, Elections Code § 142.007): statewide office — **1% of the total vote received by all candidates for governor in the most recent gubernatorial general election**. For the 2026 cycle that was **81,030** (1% of the 2022 gubernatorial vote). District, county, or precinct office — the **lesser of 500 or 5%** of the total gubernatorial vote in that territory in the most recent gubernatorial general election; if that 5% figure is under 25, the requirement is the lesser of **25 or 10%** of that vote.
- Signature gathering window: **after** the primary election — or, if the office sought is involved in a primary runoff, **after the runoff** (§ 142.009). For 2026 that meant from March 4, 2026 (no runoff for the office: ~113 days to the deadline) or from May 27, 2026 (runoff involved: ~30 days to the deadline). Signers must be registered voters in the territory of the office, and must **not** have voted in the general primary or runoff primary of a party that nominated a candidate for the office sought (§ 142.009). Each signer provides signature, printed name, residence address, and date of birth or voter registration number.
- Filing deadline: two-part process (§§ 142.002, 142.005, 142.006). (1) Declaration of Intent: **November 8, 2025 to 6:00 p.m., Monday, December 8, 2025** — mandatory; a missing declaration means the application is rejected. (2) Application + nominating petition: **5:00 p.m., June 25, 2026** (the 30th day after runoff primary election day). Withdrawal deadline: **Monday, August 24, 2026**, by notarized Certificate of Withdrawal or notarized letter to the same filing authority.
- Filing fee (or fee-waiver / in-lieu signatures): **none — there is no filing-fee option for independent candidates.** All independents must submit a nominating petition (§ 142.004). (Party candidates, by contrast, may pay a fee or file a 5,000-signature petition in lieu of the fee for statewide office.)
- Party-affiliation cutoff: the candidate must **not have voted in the primary or runoff primary election of either party** (§§ 142.008, 162.003, 162.015). There is no Texas party-registration system to deregister from; the disqualifier is primary participation, not registration.
- Sore-loser rule: a person who was a candidate for nomination in a primary election is **not eligible** for a place on the general-election ballot as an independent candidate for any office for which a candidate was nominated in the primary (§ 162.015(a)). **New law:** SB 901 (89th Leg., R.S.), effective September 1, 2025 — a person who files an application with more than one political party (primary or convention) is ineligible for a place on the ballot for a general primary election, nomination by convention, and the succeeding general election as an independent or write-in candidate (§ 162.0151).
- Ballot label allowed: **"Independent"** — printed next to the name of each candidate not aligned with a political party (§ 52.067); on a party-column ballot an "Independent" column follows the party columns (§ 52.065(d)). SOS ballot-preparation guidance abbreviates it "Ind" in the legend.

## Gotchas

- ⚠️ **The declaration is the killer.** File the Declaration of Intent in the November–December window or the June application is rejected — no cure, no late filing.
- ⚠️ **The petition window collapses if there is a runoff.** No runoff for the office: ~113 days to collect. Runoff for the office: ~30 days to collect the same number of signatures, with the same June 25 deadline.
- ⚠️ **Signers are a shrinking pool.** Anyone who voted in a primary (or runoff) of a party that nominated someone for that office cannot sign — so the petition must be built from non-primary voters and voters of parties with no candidate for that office.
- ⚠️ **Statewide is the hardest independent path in the country by this measure:** 81,030 valid signatures in 2026, petition-only, and no independent for statewide office had complied since 2006 per the litigation record — treat the statewide number as a campaign-scale operation, not a paperwork step. District offices cap at 500 signatures and are the realistic entry point.
- ⚠️ **The 81,030 figure is already stale.** It is 1% of the 2022 gubernatorial vote; after the November 2026 governor's race the next cycle's 1% will be recomputed from the 2026 vote. Do not quote 81,030 for 2028.
- The candidate, like the signers, must not have voted in either party's primary or runoff (§§ 142.008, 162.003).
