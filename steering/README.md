# The Steering Group — Charter

In business, the leader recruits the talent. In politics, only parties do. The steering group exists to do that one job — **recruiting and steering** — without becoming a party.

"Steering" means steering a candidate through the *process*: the obvious, unglamorous tasks that end most independent campaigns. It never means steering their positions.

## What the steering group DOES

1. **Choose target districts** using the published method in [`../recruiting/target-districts.md`](../recruiting/target-districts.md) — and publish the scores
2. **Recruit candidates** — approach credible local leaders with a track record, and make the ask personally
3. **Vet for track record** using the [`candidate scorecard`](../recruiting/candidate-scorecard.md) — delivery history, local trust, basic integrity checks
4. **Connect each candidate to the rails** — ballot-access help, compliance setup, fundraising stack, distribution playbook
5. **Publish everything** — who was recruited, why, what support was given, at what cost

## What the steering group may NEVER do

- Set, suggest, or bargain over a candidate's policy positions
- Ask for a loyalty pledge, platform signature, dues, or a share of fundraising
- Own or hold hostage a candidate's donor list, voter data, accounts, or content
- Punish, whip, or withdraw basic process support because of a vote a candidate cast
- Register as a party, own a ballot line, or put a shared brand on candidates
- Meet in secret about candidates. Decisions and reasons are public by default.

## The exit right (the whole design in one rule)

> Any candidate may leave at any time, for any reason, taking their donors, data, lists, and materials with them — and owe nothing.

A party cannot survive its members leaving freely. A service can. That difference is the point.

## Composition (starting model, open to revision by issue/PR)

- Small: 5–9 people. Big enough to recruit across regions, small enough to be accountable by name
- Mixed by design: at least one election lawyer, one operator who has run a campaign end-to-end, one fundraiser, and people rooted in the regions being targeted
- Term-limited and publicly listed, with conflicts of interest disclosed
- No member may recruit a candidate in a race they or their business would profit from

## Steering ≠ endorsing positions

When the steering group recruits a candidate, the public statement is always the same shape:

> "We recruited [Name] because of their track record of [specific, verifiable delivery]. They are running on their own written commitment to their constituents, which is published here. We provide process support only. Their positions are theirs."

## Amending this charter

By public PR, with the discussion in the open. Any amendment that weakens the "may never do" list or the exit right should be treated as a proposal to start a party — and rejected on those grounds alone.
