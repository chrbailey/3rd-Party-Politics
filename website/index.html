The problem

A third party reproduces the exact problem it claims to fix: the same power dynamics as the Democrats and Republicans, with everyone leveraging the party for their own personal power. And running alone, an independent hits a wall that has nothing to do with ideas — 50 different ballot-access rulebooks, compliance traps, and party-owned donor lists, data, and debate access.

The model: a recruiter + rails, not a party

In business, the leader doesn't wait for talent to apply — the leader recruits it. That is the key fix in politics, and it's the only job the steering group has.

  1. Recruit like an owner. A small, publicly named steering group targets 25–50 winnable districts first and personally recruits proven operators — mayors, builders, veterans, business owners — vetted for track record, not ideology.
  2. Steer the obvious tasks. Ballot access, signatures, compliance, fundraising, distribution, back office. Steering means steering the process — never the candidate's positions.
  3. The candidate signs one document: a public Constituent Commitment, written to their voters — specific, checkable, and graded in the open.
  4. The candidate owns everything. Donors, data, lists, and accounts — and can leave at any time, taking all of it. That exit right is what keeps this from ever becoming a party.
Think Shopify, not a franchise — and Cost Plus, not a gatekeeper. The platform serves the candidate. The candidate serves the constituents. The steering charter, including the full list of things it may never do, is public in the repo.

The rails

The unglamorous work parties quietly do for their own — provided as a service, in the open, to independents.

🗳️

Ballot access

A verified 50-state database of rules, deadlines, signature targets, and fees — every claim cited to the official Secretary of State source. Now being built, state by state.

⚖️

Compliance

FEC and state registration, treasurer setup, reporting calendars, and disclaimers — the boring errors that end campaigns, handled by checklist.

💸

Fundraising

A small-donor stack from day one, with transparent, flat pricing. The candidate owns the donor list. Always.

📣

Distribution

Debate access, earned media, podcasts, and town halls — direct-to-voter reach without renting a party brand.

🧭

Recruiting

A published scorecard — track record, local trust, delivery evidence, integrity — and a public method for choosing target districts.

🗂️

Back office

Launch playbooks, staffing checklists, and operations templates a real campaign can run at 11pm before a deadline.

Non-negotiables

  • No party — no ballot line owned by us, no party brand on any candidate
  • No platform — each candidate commits only to their own constituents
  • No loyalty oath — support is a service, never obedience
  • Candidate owns everything — donors, data, lists, content, accounts
  • Process, not ideology — we steer the tasks, never the positions
  • Public by default — playbooks, scorecards, rules, and commitments are open
