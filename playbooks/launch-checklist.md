# Candidate Launch Checklist — The First 14 Days

For an independent / unaffiliated candidate who has said yes. Owned by the candidate. The steering group helps with every step — and decides none of the positions.

## Days 1–2: Become legal

- [ ] Confirm eligibility: age, residency, citizenship, and **party-affiliation cutoff** in your state (see [`../ballot-access/`](../ballot-access/))
- [ ] Confirm you are not barred by a sore-loser rule if you recently ran in a primary
- [ ] Name a treasurer (not yourself, ideally) and open a **separate campaign bank account**
- [ ] Register the campaign committee with the FEC (federal races) and/or state authority
- [ ] Get an EIN for the committee
- [ ] Calendar **every** filing, reporting, and signature deadline — then set internal deadlines 30 days earlier

## Days 3–5: Become real

- [ ] Sign and publish your [Constituent Commitment](../templates/constituent-commitment.md) — specific, checkable, dated
- [ ] Write your one-paragraph story: who you are, what you've delivered, the specific problem you're running on
- [ ] Secure domain, email, and social handles — **in the candidate's own name/accounts**
- [ ] Set up the small-donor stack (payment processing, donation page, recurring option) — candidate owns the account
- [ ] Draft the announcement: one local event, one video, one written post. No national press fantasy.

## Days 6–10: Ballot or bust

- [ ] Print the exact signature requirement, window, and circulator rules for your state/office
- [ ] Do the buffer math: valid signatures needed × 1.5 = gathering target (validity rates are brutal)
- [ ] Recruit signature captains — people who can each own a place, a schedule, and a number
- [ ] Build the tracking sheet: gathered / validated / remaining, updated daily
- [ ] Book the election-lawyer review of the petition form **before** gathering starts, not after

## Days 11–14: Distribution

- [ ] List every local outlet that reaches your voters: papers, radio, podcasts, community pages, newsletters
- [ ] Introduce yourself to each editor/host directly — you are news; act like it
- [ ] Schedule the first three town halls (small and real beats big and staged)
- [ ] Ask your first 100 supporters personally. In person counts double.
- [ ] Publish your fundraising total and your commitment scorecard page on day 14 — transparency is the brand

## The three ways launches die

1. **The missed deadline** — everything above exists to prevent this one
2. **The invalid signatures** — wrong form, wrong circulator, no buffer. Lawyer first, gather second.
3. **The party-shaped promise** — vague platform talk instead of a signed, specific commitment to your own constituents

Print this. Check boxes in pen. Campaigns are operations.
